PLYWOOD CUT LIST / 4×8 SHEETS

How to use a plywood cut calculator for 4×8 sheets and leftover offcuts

A plywood cut plan is more than square footage. The required rectangles must actually fit on the sheets or offcuts you own, with room for saw kerf, damaged edges and any grain-direction constraints.

CUT LIST BASICS

Start with the sheet size, then test every part

A standard 4×8 plywood sheet is 48 × 96 inches. List each panel by width, length and quantity. Then compare those rectangles with your full sheets and measured offcuts. Two piles can contain the same total square footage but produce very different results if one lacks a piece large enough for the biggest panel.

Saw kerf removes material on every cut, so tight layouts need clearance between parts. Visible veneer may also require a consistent grain direction, which can prevent rotating a part simply because it would fit more efficiently.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Plywood cut calculator FAQ

01

How many 4×8 sheets do I need?

Total part area is only a first estimate. The number of sheets depends on whether each required rectangle can be arranged on the sheets after kerf, edge trimming and any grain constraints.

02

Why does saw kerf matter?

The blade removes a strip of material on each cut. Ignoring kerf can make a tight layout appear to fit when it will not in practice.

03

Can plywood scraps replace a full sheet?

Yes, when the offcuts are large enough for the required parts and have the right thickness, grade, condition and face orientation.
