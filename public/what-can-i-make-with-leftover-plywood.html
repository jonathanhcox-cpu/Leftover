PLYWOOD · PROJECT IDEAS BY SIZE

What can I make with leftover plywood?

Start with the actual width, length, thickness and condition of the scraps you own. A useful project is one whose required parts physically fit those pieces—not one that merely uses the same total square footage.

Useful plywood scraps by approximate size

01

Small strips and narrow offcuts

Good candidates include drawer dividers, cleats, spacers, small brackets, labels, jigs and edge strips when the thickness and strength are suitable.

02

Roughly 12 × 12 to 24 × 24 inches

Consider small shelves, utility panels, tool backers, trays, organizers or cabinet inserts. Verify the individual cut rectangles before assuming the area is enough.

03

Quarter-sheet and larger remnants

These can support shelf sets, bench or cabinet panels, work surfaces and storage components, depending on thickness, grade, face quality and grain direction.

WHY DIMENSIONS MATTER

Two scraps with the same area can support very different projects

A 32 × 24 inch offcut and a 48 × 16 inch offcut both contain 768 square inches, but a 30 × 20 inch panel fits only the first. That is why Leftover uses piece dimensions rather than reducing every scrap to square footage.

Before cutting, account for saw kerf, damaged edges, face quality, thickness, grain direction and any structural requirements. For a cut-planning check, use the 4×8 plywood cut calculator guide and the plywood scrap calculator.
