How much wall does one roll of wallpaper cover?

By Leftover · Updated September 9, 2026

The number printed as roll area is only a starting point. Real coverage depends on how many full-height drops you can cut from the roll and whether the pattern must repeat between strips.

Calculate my wallpaper →
WHY AREA ISN'T ENOUGH

Wallpaper is installed in drops, not square-foot fragments

A roll can have leftover square footage that cannot produce another full-height strip. Wall height plus trimming allowance determines each cut length. Patterned wallpaper can require additional length so adjacent drops align, reducing the number of usable strips per roll.

That is why two rolls with the same gross area can behave differently on a real wall. Enter the dimensions printed on your roll and your wall measurements in the wallpaper calculator rather than relying on a generic coverage number.

LEFTOVER ROLLS

A partial roll may still cover a useful small wall

Measure the remaining roll length instead of estimating by feel. If it can supply enough full-height drops, it may work for a narrow accent, alcove, closet section or repair. Pattern match, dye lot and condition still need to agree.

ONE ROLL, COUNTED IN DROPS

Example: a 20.5-inch × 33-foot roll

Use the dimensions on your own packaging; roll sizes and single-roll or double-roll sales units vary. This example roll is 20.5 inches wide and 33 feet long, or 396 inches. Its gross area is 20.5 × 396 ÷ 144 = 56.375 square feet.

For an 8-foot wall, each installed drop is 96 inches tall. With 4 inches of total trimming allowance, each blank must be at least 100 inches long. With no pattern matching, the roll yields three full drops: round 396 ÷ 100 down to 3. The remaining 96 inches cannot make another 100-inch blank.

Three drops span 61.5 inches of wall width. At 8 feet high, that is 41 square feet of wall coverage before corner details or damaged paper are considered. The difference from 56.375 square feet is not a calculation error: trimming and the unusable-for-this-wall remainder consume some paper.

For the repeat examples, round the 100-inch minimum blank up to the next full pattern repeat: 120 or 144 inches. This is a simplified allowance method. Extra paper may be needed to establish the starting motif. Half-drop or offset patterns require their own layout; follow the paper’s matching instructions rather than treating every pattern as straight match.

Turn drops into the number of rolls

A 10-foot-wide wall is 120 inches wide. At 20.5 inches per drop, round 120 ÷ 20.5 up to six drops. At three usable drops per roll, that needs two example rolls before any spare-strip reserve. At two drops per roll, it needs three.

Count corners and separate wall sections when planning strip placement. Do not automatically subtract a window’s entire area: the strips above and below it still need appropriate lengths and pattern alignment. A detailed layout may reuse those shorter pieces, but a gross-area subtraction can promise savings you cannot cut.

What to enter in the calculator

  1. Measure the total wall width and the greatest required wall height.
  2. Read the roll width and actual roll area from the label. Keep inches, feet and square feet in their matching fields.
  3. Enter the pattern repeat and total trimming allowance for one drop.
  4. Add a spare-strip reserve appropriate to your project and measure partial rolls separately.

The wallpaper planner checks those inputs together. Keep remnants from the same dye lot for future repairs; a later roll with the same pattern name may not be an exact color match.
