DECKING LEFTOVER PLANNING

Estimate what your leftover deck boards can cover

Measure the remaining deck or repair area and enter the actual board width, length and quantity you have. Leftover compares the project with full boards and measured remnants so you can estimate whether the stored material is useful and how much additional decking may be needed. If you are starting with the boards rather than a project, see what you can make with leftover deck boards by length. For a board-count and linear-foot walkthrough using board width and installed spacing, see the free deck board spacing calculator.

Deck layouts also depend on board direction, joist spacing, required end support, gaps, fastener placement and manufacturer rules. Weathered boards can differ in color or dimensions from new stock, and damaged ends may reduce the usable length. Treat the calculator as a material-planning tool and verify the structural layout before installation. For common scenarios, see the decking repair guide and small decking area guide.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Leftover decking calculator FAQ

01

Can leftover deck boards be used for a repair?

They can when the material, profile, thickness and condition are compatible with the existing deck and the replacement piece can be supported correctly by the framing.

02

Is deck square footage enough to count leftover boards?

Not always. Board face width, installed gaps, individual board lengths, joist locations and the exact repair shape can determine whether an offcut is actually usable.

03

Why keep a reserve of matching decking?

A small reserve can help with damaged cuts and future repairs, especially when a specific color or profile becomes difficult to match later.
