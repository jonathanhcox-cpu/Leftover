LUMBER CUT PLANNING

Check whether leftover boards can supply your cut list

For a quick estimate, enter the total linear footage needed, the standard board length and the full boards you have left. For a better fit check, add each required cut length and quantity. Leftover can compare those cuts with full boards and measured offcuts while accounting for saw kerf, which is more useful than simply adding total footage.

Board suitability still matters. Confirm actual dimensions, species, grade, straightness, moisture condition and whether defects fall inside a required cut. A pile with enough total length can still fail when no individual piece is long enough. Use the result to plan what to buy and keep reusable remnants for another project. WORKED EXAMPLE / LUMBER

Example: two 8-foot boards for a small bench frame

A simple frame needing two 4-foot rails and four 1.5-foot supports contains 14 finished feet of cuts. Two 8-foot boards contain 16 raw feet, but total footage alone is not enough: the cuts have to be assigned to individual boards and saw kerf has to fit between them. A workable allocation is one 4-foot rail plus two 1.5-foot supports from each board, leaving roughly 1 foot per board before kerf and end cleanup. Use the cut-list planner to test your actual stock.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Lumber leftover calculator FAQ

01

Is total linear footage enough to know whether leftover lumber will work?

No. Ten feet of required cuts cannot always come from ten feet of leftover stock because the individual board lengths may not fit the cut list. Piece-by-piece fit matters.

02

How are lumber offcuts counted?

Enter the usable lengths you actually have and compare them with the required cuts. Allow for saw kerf, damaged ends and any sections that should not be used.

03

Can leftover lumber be used for structural work?

Only when the species, grade, dimensions, condition and project requirements are appropriate. The calculator checks material fit, not structural engineering or code compliance.
