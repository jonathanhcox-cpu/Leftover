What can I make with leftover wallpaper?

By Leftover · Updated September 9, 2026

Start with the width and usable length of your remnant. Large pieces can cover a narrow wall; smaller pieces can become panels, drawer backs or repair stock without pretending every scrap is interchangeable.

PROJECTS BY REMNANT SIZE

Useful ways to reuse wallpaper

01

Narrow accent areas

A partial roll may cover an alcove, closet wall, cabinet back or narrow feature section if it yields enough full-height drops.

02

Furniture and storage details

Smaller remnants can line drawer bottoms, shelf backs or framed panels. Flat projects are often more forgiving than trying to combine fragments on a full wall.

03

Future repairs

Keep matching paper from the original dye lot for damaged strips or patches. Label the pattern, lot and room so the remnant is useful later.

CHECK THE FIT

Pattern repeat can turn apparent surplus into shortage

Wallpaper must produce complete drops. Pattern matching and trimming can consume length that a square-foot estimate misses. Use the leftover wallpaper calculator to check your actual roll and wall dimensions.

MEASURE BEFORE YOU CUT

Example: a 20.5 × 72-inch remnant

A 20.5-inch-wide remnant that is 72 inches long contains 10.25 square feet of paper: 20.5 × 72 ÷ 144 = 10.25. It still cannot make a full-height drop on an 8-foot wall because the wall alone is 96 inches high. The length is the limiting dimension.

For a smaller project, consider two flat cabinet-back panels, each 18 × 30 inches. In this example, allow a half-inch trimming margin on every edge, making each blank 19 × 31 inches. Two blanks laid end to end use 62 inches of length and fit inside the 20.5-inch width. That leaves 10 inches of roll length plus a narrow side offcut.

This layout assumes you do not need to align a repeating motif between the two panels. If both panels must start at the same point in the pattern, lay them out together first and account for the extra length needed to find the next matching motif. A damaged edge can also reduce the usable width below 19 inches.

Prepare the surface and preserve useful scraps

  1. Unroll the remnant and measure sound paper, excluding torn edges, creases and stains. Note the pattern direction.
  2. Make a paper template of the surface. Fit it before transferring the outline to your wallpaper.
  3. Check the wallpaper maker’s instructions for surface preparation and adhesive. Peel-and-stick, pasted and nonwoven papers do not all install the same way.
  4. Test a hidden area when applying paper to painted furniture. Adhesive can lift an existing finish when removed.
  5. Keep the labeled remainder from the same pattern and dye lot for repairs. Store it dry, away from sunlight.

Can I use wallpaper inside a drawer?

A removable liner can be a useful small project. Cut a template to the drawer bottom first, then check whether the paper curls or catches when the drawer is used. Wallpaper is not automatically washable, food-safe or abrasion-resistant; choose a surface and use that suit its finish. A framed panel avoids much of the wear a frequently used drawer receives.

For walls, compare complete drops in the one-roll coverage example. For small panels, width and length are usually more useful than a single square-foot total.
